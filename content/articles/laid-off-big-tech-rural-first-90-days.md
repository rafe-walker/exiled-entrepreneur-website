A laid off tech first 90 days checklist is mostly deadlines: 60 days to elect COBRA, 60 days to pick a Marketplace plan, 60 days to complete an indirect 401(k) rollover you should not do in the first place, one weekly unemployment certification you cannot skip, and a job-search log the state can audit. The emotional part is real and I am not going to write about it here. This is the administrative part, in the order the clocks start, with Arizona as the worked example because that is where I live.

I was a support engineer at Ring for about ten years, from the startup years through the Amazon acquisition, promoted from Support Engineer I to IV, and was laid off in 2026. I live off-grid in Cochise County and run several small businesses on the side. Where a detail below is mine and I have not pulled the document, it is marked **[VERIFY]**. Everything about the rules is linked to the agency that wrote them.

## Day 0–7: the paperwork that starts clocks

**Read the separation agreement for dates, not feelings.** Three dates matter: the notification date, the last day of employment, and the date health coverage ends (often the end of the month after the last day, not the last day itself). Every deadline below runs from one of these. **[VERIFY: Joshua's notification date, separation date, and coverage-end date, if he wants to publish them]**

**Severance is wages.** The IRS treats severance as wages subject to Social Security, Medicare, federal income tax withholding, and FUTA, and the withholding rate on supplemental wages is 22% (37% above $1 million for the year) ([IRS Publication 15](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15)). If you are in a higher bracket, 22% withholding under-withholds and you owe at filing; if you are in a lower one for the year because the job ended early, it over-withholds and you get it back in April. Either way, do not spend the gross.

**WARN notice.** Large employers conducting mass layoffs are subject to the federal WARN Act's advance-notice requirement; the Department of Labor publishes employer and worker guides and the regulations at 20 CFR Part 639 ([DOL WARN Act](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/layoffs/warn)). If you were part of a large reduction, check whether your notice period and pay-in-lieu line up with it. I am not stating the thresholds here because the DOL overview page defers to the statute; read the worker's guide.

**File for unemployment the week your employment ends.** Not before, not a month later. Details in the Arizona section below.

## Day 0–60: health insurance, two parallel clocks

You have two options and both have a 60-day window.

**COBRA.** Applies to group plans at employers with 20 or more employees. You get at least 60 days to elect, counted from the later of the election notice or the date coverage would end. Coverage after a job loss runs up to 18 months. The plan can charge up to 102% of the full premium. The first payment is not due at election; the plan must allow at least 45 days after election for it, and later payments have a 30-day grace period ([DOL: An Employee's Guide to Health Benefits Under COBRA](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/an-employees-guide-to-health-benefits-under-cobra.pdf), [DOL COBRA overview](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/health-plans/cobra)).

The 60 + 45 structure is a tool: you can wait to elect until near day 60 and pay within the 45 days after that. COBRA coverage, once elected and paid, generally applies back to the date group coverage ended rather than from the election date, which is what makes waiting possible; your plan's election notice states exactly how it handles this, so read it before relying on the gap. If something happens in the window, you elect and pay. If nothing does and a Marketplace plan is starting, you let it lapse.

**Marketplace (healthcare.gov).** Losing job-based coverage is a qualifying event. You can enroll up to 60 days before coverage ends and up to 60 days after; if you enroll before it ends, the new plan starts the first of the next month with no gap. COBRA typically has no premium subsidies, while Marketplace plans may qualify for premium tax credits based on income, and declining COBRA does not disqualify you from those credits ([CMS: Losing job-based coverage](https://www.cms.gov/marketplace/outreach-and-education/losing-job-based-coverage.pdf)).

For a year where income drops sharply after the separation, the Marketplace with a credit is often cheaper than 102% of a big-tech plan. Run both numbers in week one. **[VERIFY: which route Joshua took]**

## Day 0–90: the 401(k), and why "indirect" is a trap

Options are leave it, roll to an IRA, roll to a new employer plan, or cash out. The rules that bite:

- **Direct rollover (trustee-to-trustee)**: the plan sends the money straight to the IRA or new plan. No withholding, no deadline pressure.
- **Indirect rollover**: the plan pays *you*. It must withhold 20% for federal tax, and you then have 60 days to deposit the *full* amount, including the 20% you did not receive, into the new account, or the shortfall is a taxable distribution and possibly subject to the 10% early-distribution tax ([IRS: Rollovers of retirement plan and IRA distributions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/rollovers-of-retirement-plan-and-ira-distributions)).

So: direct, always. The only reason to touch the money is if you need it, and then it is not a rollover, it is a withdrawal, and you should price the tax before you do it. There is also a one-rollover-per-12-months rule on IRA-to-IRA rollovers that does not apply to direct transfers or to plan-to-IRA rollovers. **[VERIFY: whether Joshua rolled over, left the plan in place, or neither]**

## Unemployment: weekly certification, Arizona DES as the example

Every state runs this differently; the mechanics below are Arizona's. The shape is similar elsewhere: initial claim, identity verification, a waiting week, and then a weekly certification that is the easiest thing in this list to forget and the most expensive.

**Initial claim.** File with the Arizona Department of Economic Security. Identity is verified through ID.me. About a week later DES mails a Certificate of Understanding you must sign and return, and possibly a questionnaire. Direct deposit is set up at AZUI.com, and you register with Arizona Job Connection for the work-search side ([Arizona DES: Apply for UI benefits](https://des.az.gov/services/employment/unemployment-individual/apply-ui-benefits)).

**Weekly claim.** Benefit weeks run Sunday through Saturday and you must file a claim for each week you are unemployed or underemployed, confirming you still meet the requirements (same DES page). Miss a week and you do not get paid for it. Put it on a recurring calendar alarm for the same morning every week.

**Work search.** Arizona reinstated its requirement in May 2021: at least four work-search activities per week, on four different days, logged with the type of contact, the person contacted, and the employer's phone and address; DES audits, and a downloadable form (UB-101-A) exists for tracking ([ABC15 on Arizona work-search rules](https://www.abc15.com/news/state/changes-to-arizona-unemployment-benefits-how-to-prove-your-job-search-history)). Keep the email confirmations from every application; they are your audit evidence.

**Amounts and duration.** Secondary sources put Arizona's 2026 maximum weekly benefit at $320 for up to 24 weeks, with a one-week unpaid waiting week that you still have to file for ([savingtoinvest Arizona UI summary](https://savingtoinvest.com/arizona-unemployment-benefits/), [theunemployment.org Arizona guide](https://theunemployment.org/arizona/)). Those two sources disagree with each other on the minimum benefit and on how partial earnings reduce the payment, so I am not repeating either figure; the authoritative numbers are in the "A Guide to Arizona Benefits" handbook DES mails you. Read it the day it arrives.

**Side income must be reported.** The weekly claim asks whether you worked or earned any income, gross, before taxes. If you run a side business, that question is not optional and the answer changes your payment. Which brings up the next section.

## Keeping the side businesses compliant while claiming

If you have an LLC that sells things, three obligations continue through the layoff:

1. **Report the income to UI honestly.** Self-employment income reduces or eliminates the weekly benefit under your state's rules, and full-time self-employment can disqualify you. Misreporting is the fast way to an overpayment notice with penalties. Keep the business books weekly so the number is ready every Sunday.
2. **State business filings.** Arizona does not require an LLC annual report with the Corporation Commission, one of the few states that does not; what continues is the Transaction Privilege Tax license and filings with the Department of Revenue if you sell taxable goods or services, plus income tax on the pass-through ([LLC University on Arizona LLC requirements](https://www.llcuniversity.com/arizona-llc/annual-report/)). Digital products and services have their own TPT treatment; confirm yours with ADOR rather than assuming.
3. **Separate the money.** Business receipts into the business account, owner draws out of it, and nothing that makes a severance deposit look like business revenue to a future auditor.

## The job search: one application a day, logged

The system I use is deliberately boring, because the alternative is bursts of twenty applications followed by two weeks of nothing.

- **One real application per weekday.** Tailored, with a cover note that references the actual role. Twenty a week sprayed from a template is worse than five that were read.
- **Log every one** in a sheet: date, company, role, link, contact, response, next action. This is also your UI work-search log, so the two obligations share one spreadsheet. Four of these a week on four different days satisfies Arizona's rule with a day to spare.
- **Follow-ups on a 7-day cadence.** The next action column is the whole point of the sheet.
- **Anchor the résumé in what you actually did.** For me that is executive escalations, DevOps support, on-call, outage communications, Splunk/Tableau/Redshift/AWS, and a team that built in-app Wi-Fi and network self-diagnostics for customers. Specific systems and specific outcomes, not "passionate about."
- **Rural adds a filter.** "Remote" has to mean remote from a satellite link in a county with no fiber on your road, so the application list skews to companies that already run distributed teams. Before you accept anything, make sure the power and connectivity at home can hold a workday: [the off-grid power budget](/articles/remote-work-off-grid-power-budget) and [rural internet in Cochise County](/articles/rural-internet-remote-work-cochise-county) are the two checks.

## The laid off tech first 90 days checklist, condensed

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Read separation letter for three dates; file UI the week employment ends; set weekly certification alarm; open the job log |
| Weeks 1–2 | Price COBRA vs Marketplace; if Marketplace, enroll before coverage ends so there is no gap |
| Weeks 1–4 | Initiate a direct 401(k) rollover if moving it; never take a check |
| Weekly | Certify; log four work-search actions on four days; reconcile business books; report side income |
| By day 60 | COBRA election decision made (or deliberately not made, with the 45-day payment window understood); Marketplace window closes |
| Month 3 | Review severance withholding against projected annual income; adjust estimated taxes if the side businesses are earning |

None of this gets you a job. It keeps the job search from being interrupted by a lapsed insurance policy, an overpayment notice, or a tax bill you did not see coming. Those are the things that actually derail the first 90 days.
